# Strategy review — 21 Sep 2026

Full review of every engine that produces a trading signal, against its own
measured evidence. Written as a work list; each item says what to change and
what evidence justifies it.

**Headline: no engine in this repo currently has a measured edge.** Every
strategy that has been replayed loses money. The work below is therefore
ordered as (1) stop the bleeding, (2) make the measurements trustworthy,
(3) exploit the two signals that do show real predictive power. Adding more
patterns is not on the list and would not help.

---

## Evidence as it stands

| Engine | Sample | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Math engine (live paper agent) | 67 closed trades | 50.7% win, **−₹17,288**, PF 0.56 |
| Math engine (backtest, 4 configs) | 76–126 trades each | PF **0.29–0.43** — all four lose |
| Intraday engine (5 patterns) | 8 sessions × 51 stocks | every pattern ≈ **0R**, −0.055% to −0.13%/trade |
| Positional engine (8 patterns) | 130 sessions, whole board | only flag 1.60×, RSI 1.43× lift; median fwd returns negative |
| Options analyzer / pullback | 446 signals, 25 days | PF 0.98 / 1.05 — inside noise |

## A. Critical — correctness, do these first

### A1. The live agent's R:R gate measures a trade it never takes

`strategy/math_decision_strategy.py:250`

    rr = _round2(target_pts / risk)      # target_pts = 20

but `strategy/intraday_agent.py:298` exits the whole lot at `partial`
(entry + 10). So `MIN_RR = 1.0` admits any trade with `risk <= 20`, while the
trade actually taken is `10 / risk` — as low as **0.5R**.

The live book confirms it exactly: avg win ₹657, avg loss ₹1,201,
**payoff ratio 0.55**. Breakeven needs a **64.6%** win rate; the engine
delivers 50.7%. The strategy is structurally negative regardless of signal
quality.

**Do not just switch the exit to `target`.** The backtest already tested all
four combinations and every one loses:

| exit | gate | n | win% | PF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| partial | off | 113 | 36.3 | 0.43 |
| partial | ≥1.0 | 126 | 31.7 | **0.36** ← what runs live |
| target | off | 76 | 18.4 | 0.29 |
| target | ≥1.0 | 101 | 16.8 | 0.32 |

**Action:** keep the agent disarmed. Compute `rr` from the field the agent
actually exits on so the gate stops lying, and register the engine as
SUSPENDED in `models/strategy_registry.py` rather than tuning it. Note the
MIN_RR gate *worsens* results (0.43 → 0.36) — it was sized on 67 trades and
does not survive the larger sample. Remove or re-derive it.

### A2. ~45% of backtest trades are unobservable

Every run reports 91–102 trades excluded because the option's minute data ran
out before a real exit. Conclusions are drawn from roughly half the sample,
and the missing half is not random — illiquid strikes are exactly where fills
are worst.

**Action:** report PF with those trades included at a pessimistic fill as a
sensitivity band. If the two numbers disagree, the result is not usable.

### A3. Intraday costs are understated ~3×

`intraday_scanner.COST_PCT = 0.0005` (0.05% round trip). Realistic NSE
intraday equity: brokerage ~0.06% + STT 0.025% (sell) + exchange/GST/stamp
~0.02% ≈ **0.105% before slippage**, and a breakout entry on a 5-minute bar
slips at least 0.05%. True cost ≈ **0.15–0.20%**.

Every intraday pattern already nets −0.055% to −0.13% per trade. At realistic
costs they are all roughly **0.1% worse**. The scorecard is optimistic and
still negative.

**Action:** set `COST_PCT = 0.0015` and add an explicit `SLIPPAGE_PCT`.
Re-run before drawing any conclusion from the scorecard.

### A4. `box` and `vwap` re-trade the same setup 4–5× per day

`intraday_scanner.replay_stats` dedups on `(pattern, state, entry)`. For `orb`
and `level` the entry is a fixed price, so dedup works (440 and 286 signals ≈
1 per symbol-session). For `box` and `vwap` the entry drifts every bar, so
dedup never fires: 1,984 and 1,538 signals over 408 symbol-sessions ≈ **4.9
and 3.8 per symbol per day**.

Consequences: the effective sample is ~400 not ~2,000, so confidence intervals
are about twice as wide as they look; and live, the same setup reappears every
bar with a slightly different entry.

**Action:** dedup on `(pattern, state, symbol, session)` — one trade per setup
per session — and apply the same suppression in the live scan.

## B. Where the edge actually is

### B1. The 1–10 score does not work — volume does

Measured on the positional replay (large sample, fully offline reproducible):

| Pattern | score<6 | score 6–7 | score 8+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| horizontal (n=1070) | 16.0% | 22.2% | 16.7% |
| flag (n=201) | 31.6% | 28.4% | 42.9% |
| rsi (n=210) | 22.5% | 44.0% | — |

Non-monotonic everywhere, and on median forward return the *higher* score is
worse (horizontal −0.07% → −0.24% → −0.65%; flag score 8+ is −1.83%, its worst
bucket). The score sums unrelated booleans, so a high score means "passed many
checks", not "more likely to work".

Volume ratio, by contrast, is consistent and monotonic:

| Pattern | vol < 1.5× | vol ≥ 1.5× |
|---|---|---|
| horizontal | 12.7% | **22.7%** |
| triangle | 20.8% | **33.3%** |
| flag | 29.1% | **34.1%** |

**Action:** stop ranking by score. Either rebuild it by fitting weights to
measured outcomes (logistic regression on the replay, walk-forward), or drop
it and rank by volume ratio plus relative strength. Until then, label it in
the UI as "checks passed", not a quality ranking.

### B2. Two entry signals genuinely predict direction — the exits destroy them

Against a same-universe baseline of 8.0% (long) / 6.8% (short) for a +0.5%
move within 30 minutes:

- `level` (prev-day high/low break): 17.1% → **2.36× lift**
- `orb` (opening-range break): 12.0% → **1.63× lift**

Both still lose money (−0.117% and −0.081% per trade). The direction call is
real; the stop/target structure wrapped around it is not.

**Action:** this is the most promising thread in the repo. Treat the exit as
the thing to fix, not the signal.

### B3. Targets ask for most of a day's range, in two hours

Median daily high–low range for NIFTY 50 names is **1.66%** (p25 1.49%,
p75 1.85%). The engine's targets — one opening-range height beyond the break,
or 1.5× a 6-bar swing — routinely ask for 1.0–1.5%. That is 60–90% of an
entire day's range, demanded *after* the move has already started, inside the
2-hour replay cap.

That is why `box` reaches target first only 40% of the time and `vwap` only
16.6% while stopping out 44%.

**Action:** derive stops and targets from data instead of ratios. Record MFE
and MAE (maximum favourable / adverse excursion) for every replayed signal,
then set the target at roughly the 60th percentile of MFE and the stop just
beyond the 75th percentile of MAE. This is the single highest-value change
available and it uses signals already shown to have directional edge (B2).

### B4. The intraday entries chase; Pre Market's own doctrine says not to

`detect_vwap` enters at `h[-3:].max()` — the highest high of the last three
bars — immediately after a VWAP cross, with the stop at the 6-bar low. That
buys the extreme of the move with a wide stop, which is why it has the worst
stop-first rate (44%).

The Pre Market tab already implements the opposite and better-founded rule
from the ChartBank material: *don't chase the close, wait for the dip into the
25–50% zone, stop below the low only.* The Intraday Engine contradicts it.

**Action:** add a pullback entry variant (enter on a retrace toward VWAP or
the broken level rather than at the extension high) and replay it head-to-head
against the chase entry. Cheap to test, and consistent with the rest of the
app.

## C. Methodology — so results can be trusted

### C1. Nothing is out-of-sample

Intraday stats come from 8 sessions; positional from one 130-session market
phase. Thresholds (`OR_MIN_RANGE`, `BOX_MAX_DEPTH`, `VWAP_MIN_OTHER_SIDE`,
`NEAR_TRIGGER`, …) were chosen by hand and evaluated on the same data. With
5 intraday and 8 positional patterns plus their thresholds, some will look
good by chance alone.

**Action:** split train/test by date before tuning anything. Adopt a rule:
no threshold change ships unless it holds on held-out sessions. Report the
number of configurations tried alongside any positive result.

### C2. The intraday replay is not reproducible offline

`intraday_service` keeps frames in memory only, so the scorecard can only be
produced by a live backend with broker sessions, and it is lost on restart.
Research cannot be repeated or audited.

**Action:** persist each session's 5-minute bars to `data/intraday_cache/`
(same pattern as `data/eod_store.py`). This also removes most of the
rate-limit pressure in C3.

### C3. Same-day bars depend entirely on AngelOne, which rate-limits hard

Confirmed live on 21 Sep: mStock served 14 sessions of history for all 51
symbols in ~20s but returned **no bars for the current session**; AngelOne is
the only same-day source and refused roughly half the requests at ~1 req/s.
26 of 51 symbols refreshed on the first cycle.

Mitigations are in (adaptive 1.2–3.0s spacing, mStock skip-and-reprobe, stale
symbols excluded from the scan), but the ceiling is real: a full 51-symbol
cycle cannot reliably complete inside a 5-minute bar.

**Action:** either shrink the live universe to ~15–20 names that actually
produce setups, or build today's 5-minute bars from the tick collector's own
`minute_candles` (already in the DB, no rate limit) and use the brokers only
for history. The second is strictly better and removes the dependency.

### C4. `_simulate` silently drops instant winners

Trades whose next-bar open has already passed the target are skipped
entirely. This biases measured performance *downward* — conservative, but it
means `avg_r` understates reality and the skipped set is not reported.

**Action:** count them as fills at the open and report how many there were.

## D. Smaller items

- **Positional engine has no trade simulation.** It reports hit-rate lift but
  never applies its own stop/target, unlike the intraday engine. Flag shows
  1.6× lift but an 11% rate of closing 10% lower — the P&L may well be
  negative. Reuse the intraday `_simulate` so both engines are judged the same
  way.
- **`hammer` (intraday) has n=8.** Not measurable. Either widen the rules or
  mark it explicitly as unvalidated in the UI.
- **`cup` (n=9) and `ipo` (n=3)** in the positional scorecard are likewise
  decoration. The UI already flags small samples — keep that discipline.
- **The as-of discipline is sound.** Verified: session VWAP, previous-day
  levels and time-of-day volume baselines are all built from strictly earlier
  bars, and `completed_only` is applied before every scan. No look-ahead found
  in the detectors. This is the part of the codebase to preserve.

## Suggested order of work

1. **A1** — stop the live agent lying about R:R; keep it disarmed (correctness + capital)
2. **A3 + A4** — honest costs and dedup, so the scorecard means something
3. **C3** — build today's bars from our own ticks; removes the rate-limit ceiling
4. **B3** — MFE/MAE study to size stops and targets from data
5. **B2 + B4** — apply those exits to `level` and `orb`, and test pullback vs chase entry
6. **B1** — refit or retire the score
7. **C1** — train/test split before any further tuning

Items 1–3 are correctness and infrastructure: they make every later number
trustworthy. Items 4–5 are the only place in this repo where measured edge
currently exists. Item 6 removes a ranking that is actively misleading.

A realistic expectation: the direction signals in B2 are worth roughly
1.6–2.4× the base rate over 30 minutes. That is a real but modest edge, and it
survives only if costs and exits are sized to it. It will not produce a high
win rate — a 40% win rate at 2R is a good outcome and would be a large
improvement on anything measured here.
